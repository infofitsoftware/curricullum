# Module 2 Assessment — Entrepreneurial & Product Thinking

**Covers:** Chapters 1–3  
**Includes:** Module Exercises · Quiz · Outcome Assignment  
**Module outcome:** Students identify a real-world problem and select a potential software product they can develop throughout the course.

**Style note for teachers:** Prefer classroom discussion answers where marked. Written submissions are required for the Outcome Assignment.

# Part 1 — Module Exercises (Interactive + Written)

## A. Concept Check

1. Every useful business primarily starts from:
   - a) Random technology trends only  
   - b) A real problem or desired gain for someone  
   - c) Having a logo first  
   - d) Buying the most servers  

2. A strong beginner opportunity usually has:
   - a) No users available to talk to  
   - b) Repeated pain + reachable users + buildable scope  
   - c) Global monopoly potential in week 1  
   - d) Mandatory blockchain  

3. Product thinking focuses first on:
   - a) User outcomes and value  
   - b) Maximum features  
   - c) Copying every competitor button  
   - d) Ignoring workarounds  

4. Brainstorming round 1 should prioritize:
   - a) Immediate rejection of all ideas  
   - b) Quantity of ideas before heavy judgment  
   - c) Final architecture diagrams only  
   - d) Hiring a CEO  

### True / False (one-line why)

5. Innovation always means inventing something never seen before.  
6. WhatsApp can be a workaround your product must beat in some way.  
7. Choosing three course products at once is better than committing to one.  
8. A Not-now list is a sign of weak ambition.

## B. Classroom Discussion Prompts (Teacher-facilitated)

Do in pairs/groups. Teacher cold-calls 4–5 answers.

9. Name one local business and the problem it solves.  
10. Share one complaint from campus/home and classify: **Opportunity / Not now / Not software**.  
11. Take a weak idea: “AI app for everyone.” Improve it with user + outcome.  
12. Debate: Is a QR restaurant menu innovative? Why/why not?

---

## C. Explain in Your Own Words

13. Difference between a complaint and a product opportunity.  
14. Difference between feature thinking and product thinking.  
15. Why “Uber for X” is often a weak starting point for beginners.

---

## D. Ground-Reality Problem Hunt (Written)

16. List **8 problems** you personally observed in the last 30 days.  
For each, mark:

| Problem | Who feels it? | How often? | Current workaround | Software-fit? (Y/N/Maybe) |
|---------|---------------|------------|--------------------|---------------------------|

Then star your top 3.

---

## E. Product Thinking Upgrade

17. Rewrite each weak statement into a stronger product direction:

* “I want to build a social media clone.”  
* “I want to use AI somehow.”  
* “I want an app for my college.”  

Use template:

> For [user], who struggles with [problem], success means [outcome]. V1 will [simple help].

---

## F. Brainstorm → Score (pairs)

18. Brainstorm at least 10 idea titles from your top problems.  
19. Shortlist 3. Score each 1–5 on:

* Real pain  
* Access to users  
* Narrow MVP  
* Learning fit  
* Motivation  
* Ethics/safety  

Show totals.

---

## G. AI-Assisted Critique (required process)

20. Ask AI to critique your top idea **after** you provide your own problem/user/workaround.  
Submit:

* your prompt;  
* AI summary;  
* 2 things you accepted;  
* 2 things you rejected and why.

# Part 3 — Outcome Assignment

## Title
**My Course Product Decision Pack**

## Purpose
Demonstrate the Module 2 outcome:

> Identify a real-world problem and select a potential software product to develop throughout the course.

## Deliverable
One Markdown/PDF (700–1000 words) + optional 1-page photo of brainstorm notes.

### Section 1 — Real problem evidence
* Problem statement (plain language)  
* Where you observed it (campus/home/local/work)  
* Who feels the pain  
* How often it happens  
* Current workaround  
* What goes wrong with that workaround  

### Section 2 — Opportunity rationale
Explain why this is a software opportunity using:

* frequency;
* pain;
* reachable users;
* buildability for this curriculum.

### Section 3 — Product direction (product thinking)
Write:

* target user;
* outcome sentence (what success means);  
* v1 capability (one main job);  
* Not-now list (at least 4 items);  
* software form for v1 (website/web app/mobile) + why.

### Section 4 — Choice process
Show you did not pick randomly:

* 3 alternative ideas you considered;  
* short score notes (even informal);  
* why your final choice won.

### Section 5 — Commitment statement
Exact template:

> We are building **[name]** for **[users]** to solve **[problem]** by providing **[MVP capability]** first as a **[product form]** because **[reason]**. Success for v1 means **[outcome]**.

### Section 6 — Next-week user access plan
List 2–3 real people/groups you can speak with in Module 3 and what you will ask (3 questions max).

## Constraints
* Must be grounded in lived/local reality.  
* No fake “global platform” claims.  
* AI may help polish wording only; idea and evidence must be yours. Disclose AI polish if used.  
* This product should be reusable in Modules 3–22.

## Rubric (100)

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Real problem evidence quality | 20 |
| Opportunity filters applied clearly | 15 |
| Product thinking (outcome, v1, not-now) | 20 |
| Transparent choice process | 15 |
| Clear commitment statement | 15 |
| Realistic Module 3 user-access plan | 10 |
| Clarity and honesty | 5 |

**Pass:** 70+  
**Teacher approval gate:** Assignment must be marked accepted before Module 3 project work uses this idea.

## Assignment grading tips
* Reward narrow campus/local ideas with clear workarounds.  
* Penalize generic “AI for everyone,” mega-clones, and unreachable users.  
* If decision statement is vague, return for revision (do not soft-pass).  
* Strong submissions mention real people they can actually contact.
