# Chapter 1: Understanding Your Users

## Learning Objectives

After this chapter you should be able to:

1. Define who the user is for your course product.
2. Ask basic interview questions without leading the answer.
3. Build a simple user persona from real notes.
4. Separate “user I imagined” from “user I spoke to.”

---

## 1. Why This Matters

In Module 2 you chose a problem and product direction.  
Now we check: **are we building for a real person?**

Developers who skip users often build:

* features nobody asked for;
* screens that confuse people;
* “cool tech” with no adoption.

A **product-aware developer** starts with users, then writes code.

---

## 2. Core Concepts (keep these)

### Who is the user?

**User definition:** The person who will use your product to get a job done.

Also know:

* **Primary user** — main person you design for first  
* **Secondary user** — also uses it, but not the first focus (e.g., admin)

**Example:**  
Campus event app  
* Primary: club member registering  
* Secondary: club admin managing registrations  

### Customer interview

**Definition:** A short conversation to learn about someone’s problem and current behavior.

You are not selling. You are learning.

### User persona

**Definition:** A short, realistic profile of your target user based on research notes (not a fictional celebrity).

Persona includes: name label, role, goals, pains, current workaround.

## 3. Interactive Class Flow

### Talk 1 (5 min) — Teacher
“Raise hand if you built something and later realized nobody wanted it.”  
Share 1–2 stories. Link to why user research matters.

### Activity A (10 min) — Who is YOUR user?
Each student writes for their Module 2 product:

* Primary user in one sentence  
* Secondary user (if any)  
* Where this user can be found this week  

**Teacher cold-call:** 4 students read aloud. Class asks: “Is this specific enough?”

Weak: “students”  
Better: “2nd-year students who join 2+ club events each month”

### Activity B (20 min) — Mini interview practice
Pairs: Interviewer + Interviewee (use partner’s real campus/home pain if needed).

Allowed starter questions:

1. Tell me about the last time this problem happened.  
2. What did you do then?  
3. What was most annoying?  
4. How often does this happen?  
5. If a tool helped, what would “good enough” look like?

Banned:

* “Would you use my amazing app?”  
* “Don’t you hate WhatsApp groups?” (leading)

**Debrief (5 min):** What surprised you?

### Activity C (15 min) — Tiny persona
From interview notes, fill:

```text
Persona name label: ________
Role: ________
Goal: ________
Pain: ________
Current workaround: ________
Quote (optional): "________"
```

Share 2 personas with class.

---

## 4. Common Mistakes

1. Interviewing only yourself.  
2. Asking yes/no questions only.  
3. Creating a persona from imagination with zero talks.  
4. Treating admin and end-user as the same person always.

---

## 5. AI Use (light)

AI can help turn rough notes into a cleaner persona.  
AI cannot invent your interview. Bring real notes first.

**Better prompt:**  
> “Here are my interview notes: ... Turn them into a one-page beginner persona. Do not add facts I did not write.”
