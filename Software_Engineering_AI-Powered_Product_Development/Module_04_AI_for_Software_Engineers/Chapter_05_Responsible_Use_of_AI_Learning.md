# Chapter 5: Responsible Use of AI

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

1. Explain key AI ethics issues relevant to software engineers.
2. Apply responsible AI rules in school and workplace settings.
3. Handle secrets, licensing, plagiarism, and accountability correctly.
4. Connect ethics to real cost/governance cases (Uber, Microsoft-style lessons).
5. Write a personal AI usage policy for this course and future jobs.

---

## 1. Why This Matters

AI is powerful. Power without responsibility creates:

* privacy leaks;
* biased/unfair systems;
* legal/IP issues;
* insecure software;
* budget disasters;
* loss of trust.

A hireable AI-powered full-stack developer is trusted because they use AI **responsibly**.

---

## 2. AI ethics (practical, not philosophical essay)

Focus on engineering-relevant ethics:

### A) Privacy
Do not paste personal data, customer data, or secrets into public AI tools.

### B) Honesty / authorship
Be ready to disclose AI assistance when required.  
Never claim expertise you don’t have.

### C) Fairness / bias awareness
AI outputs can reflect biased data. For user-facing decisions, review carefully.

### D) Safety & security
AI can suggest insecure code. You own the risk if you ship it.

### E) Accountability
“AI did it” is not an acceptable production excuse.

**Discussion (8 min):**  
Would you paste your company’s database password into ChatGPT to “debug faster”? Why not?

## 3. Responsible AI rules for this bootcamp

1. **No secrets in prompts** (API keys, passwords, `.env`, tokens).  
2. **Understand before commit.**  
3. **No AI on marked no-AI assessments.**  
4. **Cite/disclose when asked.**  
5. **Prefer minimal context** (need-to-know).  
6. **Test AI output.**  
7. **Respect licenses** of code/snippets.  
8. **Don’t outsource your learning** on fundamentals.

## 4. Workplace responsibility & governance

Real companies now manage AI like cloud spend:

* tool allow-lists;
* budget caps;
* logging/dashboards;
* data-handling policies;
* standardization on approved vendors.

### Revisit hard examples

* **Uber (2026):** rapid AI coding adoption → yearly AI budget consumed in months → caps/controls.  
  Ethics/professional angle: encouraging unlimited use without cost ownership is irresponsible operations.

* **Microsoft (May 2026):** internal Claude Code access reduced; shift toward first-party Copilot CLI direction.  
  Professional angle: individual preference < org strategy, compliance, and cost control.

**Activity (10 min):**  
Draft 5 AI usage rules for a 10-person startup with a fixed monthly AI budget.

## 7. Interview Preparation

**Q: How do you use AI responsibly?**  
No secrets, verify outputs, follow policy, maintain accountability, control scope/cost.

**Q: What is a risk of AI-generated code?**  
Security flaws, hallucinations, unmaintainable code, IP/license issues, skill atrophy if misused.

**Q: Who is responsible if AI introduces a bug in production?**  
The developer/team that shipped it.
